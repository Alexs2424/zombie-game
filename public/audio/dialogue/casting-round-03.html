Frankie · Preferred reference

Chuck Miller - Deep, Raspy, American

User likes the existing preview. Test whether its roughness survives pleasure, anger and quiet guilt. North Jersey specificity is still unverified.

Prepared acting test — not recorded yet
  1. Oh, I missed this. Being understood.
    Warm, greedy pleasure; breathe before the second sentence.
  2. You little shit. This was my good side.
    A startled bark, then offended vanity; not a growled slogan.
  3. He asked me to wait. I heard him. I left anyway.
    No menace. No clever finish. Plain admission.

Eve · New candidate — unreviewed

Charmion - Soft and husky

British character voice with a husky description. Reject if it stays soft or breathy through anger; the preview must earn a script audition.

Prepared acting test — not recorded yet
  1. Oh, that's indecent. Give it here.
    Private delight followed by sudden possession.
  2. You tore my sleeve. I made that. I MADE that!
    Disbelief rising to disproportionate fury; let the voice become untidy.
  3. Play it again. Please. I need to hear if it's him.
    Drop the stage voice; the request is hard to make.

Vivian · New candidate — unreviewed

Kathie - Sassy & Playful

American character voice described as sarcastic with comedic timing and spoiled attitude. Reject if it only supplies a bright commercial or generic brat voice; huskiness remains unverified.

Prepared acting test — not recorded yet
  1. Oh, he was saving this. That's adorable.
    Enjoy the theft; let the affection be outrageous but sincere.
  2. Don't sweetheart me. I'm the one who does that.
    Immediate offended interruption; warmth snaps off.
  3. I keep sending money. He keeps sending it back. I don't know what else to send.
    Lose the sales rhythm; she cannot buy an easy ending.

Voss · New candidate — unreviewed

Monty - Theatrical Aristocratic Villain

Older East Coast American theatrical villain. Test authority and pleasure; reject a frail caricature, a constant sneer or a radio monologue. The catalog age is older than Voss’s target.

Prepared acting test — not recorded yet
  1. There you are. I was beginning to feel stood up. And after all this trouble.
    Delighted host; final phrase carries a personal grievance.
  2. No? You came into my house to say no to me? ... How refreshing.
    First real crack of disbelief, then hospitality forcibly restored.
  3. I knew everything she liked. Apparently that was not the same as listening.
    A little wounded and annoyed at the truth; no villain laugh.
