## Status vocabulary

- **Established:** direction explicitly supplied or confirmed by the user.
- **Working decision:** an agent-selected direction within the user's delegated creative scope. Use it consistently until revised; it is not a claim of separate user approval.
- **Open:** an unresolved question or alternative. Do not silently turn it into canon.
- **Implemented:** behavior checked against the repository. Always distinguish this from the intended design.

## Recording changes

Before writing or implementing narrative content, read the relevant owning document. Record new creative decisions during the same task, including decisions made while coding. Update the owning document and add a short log entry explaining material changes. Remove or explicitly supersede conflicting current descriptions. Keep provider-specific generation settings and final voice IDs with their eventual asset manifests, and link those manifests here.

The user has delegated character preferences and creative development to the agent. Routine creative choices can proceed without repeated approval; keep them reviewable here. Open questions are a development backlog, not an automatic request for the user to solve them all.

## Established creative brief

- An AI-built game, including AI-generated character voices and assets.
- Online four-player co-op is the target.
- Dark noir and frightening stakes with exaggerated, outlandish, dramatic characters who can occasionally go over the top. One-liners must reflect each character’s peculiar perspective.
- Dialogue, accents, rhythm, and intonation carry character personality. Cinematic scenes are not a requirement.
- Voss is a theatrical, intimidating kingpin: charming and scary at the same time.
- Character faces should aspire toward the grounded readability of Call of Duty Zombies, while remaining original and practical for a browser game and limited budget.
- Blender is the preferred character asset workflow. Facial polish comes later.
- Dialogue must be specific to player actions and each character’s perspective, with a harder, badass noir edge. Mob of the Dead and Sin City are tonal references; writing remains original.
- Characters need extensive personal histories and human, badass personalities. Working interpretation of the latest direction: emphasize Sin City for dangerous loyalties and noir intensity, retaining Fallout as a secondary reference for eccentricity and contradiction. Keep all characters original.
- All story, character, quest, Easter egg, and related mechanics decisions must be written down.
