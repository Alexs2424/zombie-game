# Story and plot

Status: working narrative direction under the [established brief](README.md). Quest mechanics live in [quests and Easter eggs](quests-and-easter-eggs.md).

## Premise and tone

**The House Always Wins:** Vincent Voss made a bargain to keep his casino alive. Guests whose debts never closed cannot leave, even after death. Voss owns the business; the House is the supernatural counterparty, not another name for Voss.

The tone is dark noir with exaggerated, outlandish, dramatic personalities and character-driven humor. Characters can react to danger with operatic indignation, absurd confidence, or wildly inappropriate priorities. People joke to regain control, conceal fear, needle an old acquaintance, or avoid admitting guilt. Death and betrayal retain their weight. Do not interrupt a major revelation with a disposable gag or make every speaker equally sarcastic.

Current dialogue direction: original hard-boiled crime phrasing, specific responses to player actions, personal menace, and dark situational humor. Mob of the Dead and Sin City are user-supplied tonal references. See [contextual dialogue](contextual-dialogue.md) for the current writing pass. This does not add mandatory cinematics.

Working decision: the present night is in **1976**, in a fictional Las Vegas casino with older architectural grandeur and more recent alterations. It was operating until very recently; its deserted appearance reflects an evacuation and the approaching collection. The location is not initially presented as a ruin abandoned for decades. The original bargain predates this night; its precise date is open.

The casino's public brand name is still open. Last Jackpot remains the game/project title; Grand Casino and Grand Hotel remain current map labels pending a branding decision.

## Presentation

No required cinematics, camera takeovers, or dialogue-choice scenes. Tell the story through live PA announcements, crew conversations, telephone/recorded messages, documents, changes to the environment, and optional interactions. Players retain control during reveals and the ending.

Authored, prerecorded AI dialogue is the working production choice. Personality does not depend on facial animation or an in-game language model. Optional portrait reactions or facial polish may be added later.

## Story beats

1. **Closing night:** Frankie, Eve, Leon, and Vivian enter for different personal reasons. At midnight the machines pay out, the exits become unusable, and the dead rise. Establish their reasons through short exchanges and belongings, not an introductory film.
2. **The helpful owner:** Voss addresses them through the PA and offers a route out in exchange for restoring parts of his casino. Each task increases his access to its systems. Marlowe's remarks quietly challenge his account.
3. **The previous four:** a photograph and invitations behind the bar suggest that another group received the same offer. A handwritten warning reads, “Don't open the cage.” The cage refers to the secure cashier area.
4. **The missing guest:** reception records Elias Varga of room 214 as checked out, but his luggage remains. Following the existing suitcase/key/gallery investigation reveals a recent collection entry. The paperwork is concealing disappearances.
5. **The actual stakes:** evidence from the club, speakeasy, and collection records reveals wagers involving years of life, memories, and other people's futures. The zombies are uncollected or unsettled debtors. The final terminology and categories of debt need one consistent rules pass.
6. **The transfer:** Voss assembled the crew to transfer ownership and the accompanying obligation. His offer of escape is real but self-serving. Four living signatures are the current plot device; their eligibility and consent rules are not yet final.
7. **The counter-wager:** the crew learns a contractual weakness and prepares a fixed game against the House. The solution must follow previously discoverable rules rather than a new exception introduced in the finale.
8. **The Collector:** the House's enforcer tries to complete collection. Prepared mechanisms create opportunities to defeat it and resolve the wager.
9. **Freedom with a loose end:** the exits reopen. Vivian discovers a deed to another property among the stolen documents. Deliver this through gameplay, a readable prop, and dialogue. Voss's fate remains open.

## Rules currently adopted

- Voss cannot simply order the House or its Collector to stop.
- The House uses wagers, accounts, and agreements. Its horror appears through the casino's ordinary business practices.
- Restoring equipment does not mean a probability machine caused the outbreak. That alternative pitch is not current canon.
- The four protagonists become a recurring ensemble. Their disagreements should evolve into earned trust during the map.
- A failed run is a gameplay reset for now. Do not claim every restart is a literal time loop.
- The main mystery is optional for ordinary survival play. Completing it resolves this map's immediate escape problem.

## Open story work

- What Voss requested, what he knowingly surrendered, and the original bargain's exact date.
- Why these four are eligible to sign, and how that fiction works when fewer than four humans are connected. No mandatory bots or character-specific locks have been chosen.
- Varga's exact role and the identities/fates of the earlier four. Do not make him a relative of a protagonist by default.
- Marlowe's precise obligation to the House and the limits on what he can say.
- The contractual weakness, final wager, Collector identity, and Voss's final choice.
- How story completion transitions into continued survival or a results screen.

Resolve these before recording final revelation dialogue. They need not block voice auditions or the first playable investigation.
