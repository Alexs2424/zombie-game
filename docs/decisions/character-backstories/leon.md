# Leon Mercer

Working canon. Read the [cast bible](README.md) for chronology and implementation boundaries.

## A practical education

Leon is 39, Black American, raised in southeast Texas. His family did not have money for replacing something simply because it had stopped working. He learned repair through neighbors, discarded radios, and paid jobs where being right mattered less to a supervisor than getting production moving again.

He was a gifted young maintenance worker and knew it. In 1961, he accepted a temporary workaround on a loading mechanism rather than insist on a full shutdown. A colleague, **Walt Henson**, badly injured a hand when the workaround failed. Leon had followed instructions he understood were inadequate.

Walt survived, changed jobs, and built a life Leon is not the center of. Leon kept visiting with offers of help until Walt asked him to stop treating every ordinary errand as a chance to repair the accident. They still exchange short letters. The friendship is uneven, not erased.

Leon left for Las Vegas in 1963. At first he worked wherever he could find a broken appliance and a customer who might pay. Voss offered stable employment and listened while Leon explained what was wrong with the hotel's systems. It felt like respect. Some of it was respect. That made the later exploitation more effective.

## How the building became his excuse

Over the next decade, Leon became the person staff called when management's official process went nowhere. He knew which porter had a bad knee, which service door trapped fingers, and which cook would lose a shift if the refrigerator failed. He learned that threatening to quit meant threatening to leave them with the failures.

He also enjoyed being necessary. He kept fixes in his head, postponed training replacements, and quietly corrected work rather than explain it. His belief that nobody else would do the job properly became self-fulfilling.

Voss requested a separately metered circuit for the secure cash area. Leon installed it under an off-book order. He verified that it worked electrically and let someone else's assurance replace a full understanding of what it powered. The similarity to the loading accident is something he has kept out of his own account of the job.

Closing night is his final shutdown assignment. He intends to retrieve his records, disconnect what can be disconnected safely, and leave. He has told his wife that before. This time he brought the box for his tools.

The circuit serves the casino's secret operations; it is not a probability machine or a scientific cause of the House. Its precise destination awaits the quest design.

## People waiting at home

His wife **Della** is a bus dispatcher. She likes him, makes him laugh, and has no patience for a man who can schedule a generator overhaul but cannot say when he will be home. Their marriage is alive and affectionate. Its argument is not whether he cares. It is why everyone else's emergencies always get the first claim on that care.

Their daughter **Ruthie**, 11, collects weather observations and wants a radio set that picks up something stranger than the station her father likes. Leon keeps improving it instead of declaring it finished and giving it to her. Della has pointed out the pattern. He found a faulty dial to discuss instead.

He wants to open a repair shop with a listening room in the back. Della wants a specific date and an honest budget, not another beautiful description. Neither is wrong. His survival matters because there is an ordinary, unfinished life he could still choose.

## Pleasure, pride, and the difficult bits

Leon restores radios, argues passionately about bad recordings, and reads cheap detective paperbacks with improbable titles. He guesses the killer early and gets irritated when the book agrees too easily. He sings enthusiastically while working alone and stops the instant someone appears.

He is patient with sincere ignorance and ruthless with confident negligence. He is also capable of becoming the confident man nobody wants to correct. An apprentice suggesting a better method can provoke a longer explanation than the job requires.

He dislikes waste but hoards parts he will never use. He complains about poor labeling while keeping three tins marked MISC. He can hold a dangerous situation steady, then become disproportionately upset about a borrowed screwdriver returned bent.

## What makes him formidable

Leon keeps understanding cause and effect while other people fixate on spectacle. Under pressure he names the next action clearly, gets his hands on the problem, and accepts physical discomfort without turning it into a performance. He has practical familiarity with guns and tools, not a concealed elite military history.

He will destroy expensive equipment to protect a person. His tougher moral test is recognizing when the entire system should stop, even if keeping it running has been the basis of his identity. He should sometimes be the person who argues against restoring another circuit for Voss.

He is not immune to fear or persuasion. Threaten employees who depend on him and he may take a foolish personal risk. Offer to shoulder a task and he may decline until his own exhaustion endangers everyone. Let another character save him from that habit without humiliating his competence.

His arc ends with delegation and a decision to leave something broken. The crew can help him see that returning home is a responsibility, not a selfish vacation from responsibilities.

## Writing and delivery

Use concrete observations before conclusions. His southeast Texas cadence can stretch with patience and sharpen suddenly when somebody ignores a warning. Let an occasional furious speech run away from him, then allow him to notice he is arguing with a dead machine.

Conversational examples:

> “Della wants a date for the shop. Turns out ‘soon’ isn't a month.”
>
> “No, leave that one. I labeled it wrong. ... Yes, I heard myself.”
>
> “I knew the fix wouldn't hold. I was hoping it would hold long enough to be somebody else's shift.”

The last line is about the loading accident, disclosed only in a quiet personal exchange. It does not accuse another character of causing it.

Potential evidence: a half-finished shop estimate, a message asking him to come home, and a revised work order. These reveal priorities without requiring family voice performances or off-map scenes.
