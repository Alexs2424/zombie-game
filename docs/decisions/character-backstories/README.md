## Human texture and tone

The cast can be extravagant, vain, funny, vicious, sentimental, and wrong. Competence earns the badass moments. A character's expensive line lands because the player just watched them do something difficult, not because every ordinary action receives an aphorism.

Give each person ordinary wants and different conversational speeds. Friends interrupt, mishear, answer the wrong question, revive an old argument, or say something embarrassingly plain. Sometimes a response is just “Yeah,” “Give me a minute,” or “Don't tell him I said that.” Silence also communicates.

Surviving relatives and friends have their own work, wishes and boundaries. They are not all murdered, secretly tied to the House, or waiting to forgive the protagonist. Do not use an identity or accent as a substitute for individual experience. Not every souvenir needs a supernatural function.

The supporting people named here are writing resources. They do not require additional character models, voice actors, rooms, quest steps, cinematic scenes, or live AI conversation. A letter, invoice, disagreement, or unremarked habit can carry a history. Only add physical evidence to the map deliberately and document its placement.

### The proprietor and the bartender

- **Voss / Frankie:** employer, benefactor, and a deliberate substitute for the approval Frankie wanted at home. Voss calls coercion loyalty. Frankie must stop treating Voss's approval as evidence he is a decent man.
- **Voss / Eve:** she values that he once put her name on the building. He expects her gratitude to outweigh her judgment. He praises her performance when she is asking about a missing person, and it is infuriating.
- **Voss / Leon:** genuinely admires Leon's work and deliberately exploits his duty to other employees. Threatening innocent people is most effective when disguised as a maintenance deadline.
- **Voss / Vivian:** appreciates nerve and wants her to feel like the clever exception. She initially likes being recognized by the most dangerous person in the room; later she sees that flattery as the hook.
- **Marlowe / Frankie:** knew him before the expensive suit. Refuses to call his violence noble, but knows when Frankie needs water more than advice.
- **Marlowe / Eve:** watched her rehearse in an empty lounge. Remembers Art as a person with a favorite drink, not merely the missing half of an act.
- **Marlowe / Leon:** have worked late shifts together and loan each other ordinary tools. Each knows how the other uses work to postpone a decision.
- **Marlowe / Vivian:** recognizes a false social manner without necessarily knowing her real identity. He will not reward flirting with indiscretion. He also will not embarrass someone solely to prove he can.
- **Voss / Marlowe:** the longest and most intimate relationship on the map. They have actual affection and ordinary shared memories. Marlowe's ability to remember an earlier, less magnificent Voss is both comforting and intolerable to him.

## Reveal pacing

1. **Early survival:** let personality establish itself through decisions, observations, and practical reactions. Players need names and immediate motives, not six chronological biographies.
2. **First quiet opportunities:** reveal a mundane detail, a relationship, or an opinion that does not advance the central mystery. It makes the later confession belong to a person.
3. **Evidence contradicts self-image:** expose something the speaker has omitted. Give other characters a response; do not deliver six uninterrupted confessions.
4. **A choice costs something:** show changed behavior during gameplay. Protecting someone, sharing evidence, or refusing an instruction matters more than a declaration of redemption.
5. **Afterward:** leave consequences unresolved where appropriate. Surviving the House does not reconcile a family, restore a livelihood, or erase a victim.

No survival-critical information is locked behind optional personal banter. Side memories should respect current quest knowledge, danger, character presence, and anti-repetition rules in [contextual dialogue](../contextual-dialogue.md).
