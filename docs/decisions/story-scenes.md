# Playable story scenes

Status: **working treatment**, not implemented scenes or final recording scripts. [Story and plot](story-and-plot.md) owns canon; [quests and Easter eggs](quests-and-easter-eggs.md) owns progression and map integration. This document owns staging, dramatic purpose and scene dialogue. Later contract-dependent scenes are explicitly provisional.

## How the story plays

The night takes place in 1976. Four people arrive with private business; Voss turns their need to escape into work for him. Evidence gradually turns an apparently helpful host into a known accomplice. The crew's emotional victory is choosing each other despite having good reasons to leave each other behind.

A scene is a conversation or discovery during play. Players keep their movement, aim and camera. No forced lineup, camera cut or cutscene is required. A photographed face, an interrupted announcement and a suitcase left behind can carry more weight than a large spectacle.

The preferred route below is an order of revelations, not a requirement to discover every object in sequence. Keep evidence found early. Do not speak knowledge-dependent dialogue until its prerequisites are shared. Optional survival players can ignore the investigation.

Scene IDs identify these treatments; quoted lines use their speaker and sequence within the scene until a production manifest assigns individual asset IDs. All dialogue here is new, unrecorded working text. Never automatically record this entire document as a finished script.

## scene.01 — Closing time

**Location / trigger:** central casino, opening of a fresh run. New authored midnight event; exact spawn logistics remain an implementation choice.

**Staging:** a brief payout rattles through the machines, then stops too cleanly. The building's usual room tone drops away. The first dead guest is close enough to be recognized as a person in evening clothes. Players discover the unusable exits themselves; do not turn an actual input failure into the narrative effect.

**Purpose:** establish four incompatible priorities before exposition. They have come to collect money, retrieve a friend's effects, address suspicious building work, and steal access to the vault. The House has trapped them in the same problem.

**Possible opening exchange, only when those speakers are present:**

- Frankie: “Voss owes me money. Nobody burns the place down till I get it.”
- Vivian: “How much? I need to know how careful we're being.”
- Eve, after the first corpse gets up: “That man is not in my act.”
- Leon: “Then stop watching him and move.”

This is a pool of short reactions, not four lines that must delay the first fight. If combat starts immediately, play only the urgent call. Their private motives can surface later.

**Persistent fact:** the exits are unusable; the crew needs another way out. The actual door behavior and opening payout require implementation.

## scene.02 — The owner welcomes you

**Location / trigger:** first brief listening window after the opening danger, via PA. Voss knows the people he invited are trapped; this does not grant arbitrary surveillance of their later actions.

**Staging:** the announcement chime is tasteful and absurdly normal. Voss speaks like an attentive host correcting an inconvenience. Keep his first threat buried in courtesy.

- Voss: “My friends. An unpleasant interruption. Stay together, follow my instructions, and we can still end this evening civilly.”
- Frankie: “You said that the last time I worked late.”
- Voss: “And here you are, Frankie. A recommendation in itself.”

**Purpose:** establish history and manipulation, without announcing the supernatural rules. Voss offers instructions that eventually lead to restoring his access to systems. He does not yet admit the transfer.

**Missing-speaker fallback:** Voss's first line stands alone. Omit Frankie's exchange when absent. Short journal objective: investigate the hotel records and the route to the service supply room; do not rely on remembering a long PA speech.

## scene.03 — Four people before you

**Location / trigger:** inspect a new photograph and invitations beside Marlowe's bar. Existing shop stays usable independently.

**Staging:** four ordinary people, badly arranged for a photograph. The warning “Don't open the cage” is handwritten on the back. Let the player turn/read the item; subtitles and journal preserve its meaning.

- Marlowe: “Four of them. He had their drinks waiting.”
- Vivian: “You remember everyone's order?”
- Marlowe: “The ones I wish I'd refused.”

**Purpose:** turn hospitality into selection. Marlowe admits an uncomfortable memory rather than acting like an omniscient quest terminal. Do not name the previous four or declare their fates before those decisions are made.

**Clue:** cage means secure cashier area, clarified through signage and later evidence. The warning raises a question; it does not impose a softlock if the player already visited the public cashier hall.

## scene.04 — A guest who checked out without his luggage

**Location / trigger:** existing reception ledger, Varga suitcase, key, west panel, gallery register and cache chain. Add dialogue to the investigation rather than replacing its established reward.

**Staging:** the evidence becomes progressively less plausible: a completed checkout, possessions left behind, then an entry that is far too recent. The player's action opens both gallery ends as already designed. Room 214 is text in the clue, not a new playable guest room.

- Eve, comparing records: “A checkout without luggage. Someone finished his part for him.”
- Leon, at the collection register: “That's tonight. Somebody's still keeping these books.”
- Frankie: “Then somebody's still got a name we can ask for.”

**Purpose:** demonstrate a contradiction the player can personally verify. Avoid immediately deciding Varga's identity, assigning him to the earlier four, or claiming his killer is known.

**Recovery:** retain each document in the shared journal. A disconnected discoverer must not take the key or knowledge away. Existing solo behavior is not evidence that shared co-op behavior exists.

## scene.05 — Leon's signature

**Location / trigger:** new equipment interaction in the supply room after access through the hotel. The work order and surveillance footage are proposed additions.

**Staging:** a familiar, unshowy alteration in the wiring identifies Leon's work. A paper work order bears his sign-off. The crew restores a circuit and gains evidence; Voss also regains observation in clearly established coverage areas. Represent coverage consistently before giving him location-specific lines.

- Leon: “That's my sign-off.”
- Eve: “On what?”
- Leon: “A circuit they didn't want on the public plan. I asked what it powered. They paid me to stop asking.”
- Eve: “And did you?”
- Leon: “You're looking at it.”

**Purpose:** implicate a likable survivor without turning him into the inventor of the supernatural outbreak. His admission is plain and costly.

**Result:** shared evidence of concealed operations; new surveillance capability. Exact electrical interaction, footage content and coverage remain implementation work. No mandatory new hidden room or probability engine.

## scene.06 — The hand was chosen before the cards were dealt

**Location / trigger:** dedicated High Roller investigation using marked cards and records. Independent of the existing flush challenge and its revolver reward.

**Staging:** the player deduces a fixed result by comparing evidence. They do not repeatedly gamble until the correct random result appears. The table should look like a crime scene someone has politely reset.

- Vivian: “These cards weren't marked to win a game.”
- Eve: “They were marked to repeat one.”
- Vivian: “I hate finding out I'm somebody else's clever idea.”

**Purpose:** show ritualized business practice and engineered consent rather than a generic occult altar. Lead to the private agreement in Velvet Hour.

**Open dependency:** the actual card solution and its legal/supernatural significance must follow the final contract rules. These lines describe the intended evidence; do not ship them before the props substantiate them.

## scene.07 — The private agreement

**Location / trigger:** agreement discovered in the existing Velvet Hour speakeasy. Preserve the portrait/keypad entrance and ordinary Mystery Box access.

**Staging:** an elegant document, annotations and collection records. The first supernatural revelation should read like an accountant has itemized a human life. Let players inspect concrete examples once the debt taxonomy is finalized.

- Eve: “He didn't hide the cost. He gave it a nicer heading.”
- Leon: “Years. Names. People who never sat at the table.”
- Vivian: “I've sold some ugly things. I knew what I was selling.”

**Purpose:** reveal that Voss knowingly accepted the sacrifice of designated debtors to preserve prosperity and control. Do not explain him as innocently tricked into an ordinary business loan.

**Script hold:** Leon's exact categories depend on the unresolved rules governing years, memories and third parties. This is provisional thematic dialogue, not authority to implement those categories independently.

## scene.08 — The wrong side of the glass

**Location / trigger:** newly authored secure cashier opening after the transfer evidence becomes discoverable. Current collision-blocking glass and scenery safe are not already this quest.

**Staging:** entering the cage makes an old warning physical. Frankie recognizes the place where he escorted Martin Keene in 1973. The transfer document reframes Voss's assistance. Players need room to read before another loud fight.

- Frankie: “Martin Keene. That's who I brought in here.”
- Eve: “What happened to him?”
- Frankie: “I left. That's the part I know.”
- Voss, only if observation is established: “You did your job.”
- Frankie: “Yeah. That's what I've been calling it.”

Then the transfer discovery lands in a separate beat:

- Vivian: “He doesn't want the place back. He wants our names on it.”
- Leon: “With us inside.”

**Purpose:** confession without instant forgiveness; expose the four-signature scheme without silently resolving eligibility or magically binding the players just for inspecting a prop.

**Open dependencies:** consent, party-size fiction, ownership transfer and cage access. Do not make pressing Interact count as agreeing to a contract before the player is told what the action means. Keene's fate remains unknown.

## scene.09 — Stop doing his work

**Location / trigger:** return to Marlowe or access his discrepancy notes after learning of the transfer. New notes and conversation.

- Marlowe: “I kept a second set of figures.”
- Leon: “Why didn't you give us those first?”
- Marlowe: “Because I hoped you'd find a way out that didn't involve me.”
- Frankie: “Hope's over. Put them on the bar.”

**Purpose:** Marlowe joins the moral risk. His reluctance comes from complicity and fear, not an invented universal magical ban on speech. He can reveal a discrepancy; he cannot recite a conveniently complete solution he has always withheld.

**Result:** corroborating evidence helps the crew derive the counter-wager. The precise contractual weakness remains open. Seed it in earlier readable materials before locking this scene. No finale-only exception.

## scene.10 — Four people, one bad idea

**Location / trigger:** preparation across surveillance, power, dealer table and cashier mechanism. Flexible shared tasks; no character-exclusive puzzle stations.

**Staging:** the same building they repaired for Voss becomes the means of resisting him. The transformation is legible in machinery states, lights and sound. Completed preparation persists within the session.

- Eve: “He keeps telling us where to stand.”
- Vivian: “Then let's disappoint him.”
- Leon: “Tell me what needs to stay running. Everything else can burn out.”
- Frankie: “And nobody gets left holding the door.”

**Purpose:** express changes through choices: Eve shares control, Leon accepts deliberate imperfection, Vivian risks her exit, Frankie protects people instead of an employer.

**Open dependencies:** final wager, exact task graph and activation. The dialogue conveys commitment without inventing a working solution. An absent speaker never makes preparation impossible.

## scene.11 — Collection

**Location / trigger:** future Collector encounter in the central casino after an explicit team-ready activation policy is selected.

**Staging:** familiar lanes remain readable. The House interrupts the casino's polite presentation through machinery and sound; prepared systems expose vulnerability windows. Do not use an unskippable boss monologue over the first attack telegraph.

- Voss, at a safe opening: “There are rules even I cannot change.”
- Frankie: “Should've put that on the invitation.”
- Leon, only when an authored vulnerability actually opens: “Now! While it's open!”

**Purpose:** distinguish Voss's personal power from the House's enforcement. The Collector's identity, design and attacks are open. Defeating an encounter is not proof the crew has killed the House itself.

**Production hold:** write phase callouts after the mechanics are settled; match each call to a readable state. Never claim an attack is safe because a line ran late. Boss rules and final contractual resolution must agree.

## scene.12 — An exit, not absolution

**Location / trigger:** future successful resolution and reopened exits. Ending presentation remains playable. Whether the session ends or continues survival is unresolved.

**Staging:** returning ordinary outside sound is the first sign of freedom. Nobody delivers a speech forgiving Frankie, solving Eve's friendship or repairing Vivian's family. Their next choices still matter.

- Leon: “I need a telephone.”
- Eve: “Who are you calling?”
- Leon: “Home. Before I find another thing to fix.”

At a readable stolen deed, separate from the emotional release:

- Vivian: “There's another address.”
- Frankie: “Please tell me it's a diner.”
- Vivian: “I wish it was.”

**Purpose:** earn relief, preserve consequences, then open the next mystery. The deed does not mean Voss survived, died or became the Collector; his final fate remains open. Do not require Vivian to be selected for the deed to appear in the shared journal.

## Co-op delivery and scope

Select one eligible discoverer response for a shared event, not four simultaneous remarks. Character-specific confession scenes require that character; if absent, preserve the plot through neutral evidence and journal text without putting their confession in somebody else's mouth. Optional personal scenes may be missed without blocking the quest.

Story prerequisites use shared discovered facts, not whether the whole audio clip finished. Interrupted essentials remain readable and can have a short replay. Late joiners get an updated journal; do not restart the full welcome for the team. Any NPC remark about a private action requires plausible observation.

The first production slice remains scenes 01–04 in reduced form: welcome, bar counterpoint and the existing hotel investigation. The full scene list is a writing target, not an expanded immediate implementation commitment. Before final recording, settle contract rules, party-size fiction, counter-wager, Collector mechanics, Voss's fate and the post-ending session flow.
