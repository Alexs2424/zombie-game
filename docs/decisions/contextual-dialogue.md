## User direction and reference

Lines should respond to what the player is actually doing, be more individual, and feel badass. The user supplied [the Mob of the Dead cinematic intro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lpG7vEOlbIc) and named Sin City as inspiration. The linked video's identity was verified through its page; transcript retrieval returned no transcript. No claim of having listened to or transcribed it is made.

Use original hard-boiled crime dialogue: specific physical consequences, ruthless confidence, personal history, resentment, and dark humor. Do not reproduce reference dialogue or imitate an actor's voice. Keep the exaggerated cast, but move exaggeration into their values, decisions, and sudden reactions. They do not need a theatrical punchline after every action.

## Writing rules

- Start with an exact observable situation. Write what this particular person would think about it. The event, weapon, location, recent action and relationship should change the response.
- The character must have grounds for what they say. Do not claim a kill after a nonlethal hit, an empty inventory after one empty magazine, or a teammate rescue after ordinary nearby combat.
- Give combat lines a short, forceful landing. Save longer admissions, threats and exchanges for clear listening windows.
- Let humor emerge from an alarming belief or an uncomfortable truth. Reduce occupational wordplay and interchangeable cleverness.
- Cast members can swear when anger, pain or intimacy warrants it; constant profanity is not a substitute for a distinct voice.
- Everyday action stays mostly quiet. Rare and meaningful combinations deserve the memorable lines.
- Context-specific alternate lines compete for one dialogue slot. They are not a playlist that all plays after the event.

## Short exchanges

### A purchased shotgun — old familiarity

Trigger: Frankie's first shotgun purchase while Voss can observe him; only after establishing that they know each other. Do not also play Frankie's solo shotgun line.

- **Voss:** “Still buying the loud ones, Frankie?”
- **Frankie:** “You taught me. Make sure the next room hears.”

### A successful revival — vanity under pressure

Trigger: Leon revives Eve; no enemies within the immediate danger radius after completion. Do not delay health restoration for dialogue.

- **Eve:** “Tell me that looked deliberate.”
- **Leon:** “You fell down a staircase.”
- **Eve:** “Yes. But with authority.”

This exchange requires an actual future stair-related downing; the current game does not implement falling between floors. Keep it deferred unless that event becomes real. Never fire it after an ordinary flat-ground downing.

### Entering the luggage gallery — different instincts

Trigger: Vivian and Leon enter the opened gallery together for the first time, after reading the register.

- **Vivian:** “Dead guests. Unclaimed luggage. Finally, a sensible business model.”
- **Leon:** “Those people had names.”
- **Vivian:** “I know. They're on the tags.”

Vivian's callousness should produce friction, not make Leon the designated fool. Do not use this exchange during a personal-loss revelation.

### Metadata defaults for this bank

- **Speaker / audience:** the named local actor speaks; tactical calls reach nearby teammates according to the eventual communication design. Shared discoveries use team delivery. Do not let all clients independently select different lines for the same event.
- **Knowledge:** everyday rows need no quest revelations. Family names are personal remarks, not quest unlocks. Story-specific rows above retain their explicit prerequisites.
- **Priority:** incoming tactical need (down, empty, requested cover) outranks ordinary jokes; essential story gets a recoverable delivery slot. A funny reload line must yield to immediate gameplay information.
- **Replay:** ordinary rows inherit the proposed eight-second team spacing, three-minute per-line cooldown and two-use cap. Urgent information may need a short neutral fallback after its comic variant is exhausted. Do not suppress critical information to preserve a joke cooldown.
- **Interruptions:** cancel stale kill/reload jokes when state changes. A downed or dead speaker must not finish an irrelevant victory line. Never delay actual gameplay until speech ends.
- **Selection:** narrow combinations beat generic rows; e.g. Vivian's last-bullet round-ending line suppresses her generic round-ending line. Revive pairs beat generic thanks. Probability and tuning remain open pending in-game listening.


## Trigger implementation boundaries

Inspected source: `lib/game/simulation.ts` currently emits hit, kill, hurt, purchase, reload, dry and other gameplay events. Kill events include a headshot flag and position, but do not yet carry the full actor/enemy/attack history required here. Limb state exists; reliable severance dialogue needs a new transition event rather than firing on every hit to an already missing limb. Revives, the full quest and restored surveillance are planned content.

Working runtime design: add structured context for actor/target identity, attack cause, weapon, resulting ammunition, health before/after, severance transitions, room, shared quest knowledge, and a short recent-event history. Match specific rules before general barks. NPC reactions require plausible observation or explicit in-world communication.

Initial tuning proposals: ordinary combat barks expire after two seconds; do not queue a kill joke to play after the player is downed. Cap ordinary dialogue to one foreground line and roughly one incidental bark per team per eight seconds. Individual comic lines have a minimum three-minute repeat cooldown and a two-use-per-run cap; discoveries are once per run. These values are proposed for playtesting, not implemented constants. Urgent tactical calls and essential story information have separate rules and recovery behavior.

No new voice generation was performed for this revision. Revise audition text to this contextual pass before spending further credits on line delivery. Existing voice identity candidates can still be evaluated on timbre and accent independently.
