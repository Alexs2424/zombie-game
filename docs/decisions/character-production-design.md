## Ensemble design

A player should identify a teammate by the broad clothing shape, then recognize the same person by sleeves and hands in first person. Four playable silhouettes: Frankie's heavy tobacco shoulders; Eve's sharp ivory asymmetry; Leon's lean teal workwear; Vivian's compact plum tailoring. NPC contrast: Voss's nearly motionless midnight-blue formality against Marlowe's narrow burgundy waistcoat and working posture.

1976 is the present. Older architecture, portraits and inherited furniture can predate it. Clothes are lived in, repaired and specific to the wearer, not six matching tactical outfits. Faces should support a useful medium-distance read: recognizable nose, brow, hair mass and resting expression. Avoid making everyone equally young, symmetrical, muscular or permanently scowling.

Their exaggeration lives in priorities and performance: Frankie is offended on behalf of his suit while saving a life; Eve critiques a catastrophe as if she can dismiss it; Leon gives a lethal problem an increasingly furious diagnosis; Vivian flirts with opportunity even while bleeding. Voss can make a threat sound like a privilege. Marlowe makes an intolerable admission sound like closing-time administration.

## Frankie “Chips” Caruso — the dangerous man trying to be decent

**Face and body:** 47, Italian-American from Newark. Broad chest, substantial middle and thick neck; a heavy, forward silhouette rather than a bodybuilder. Broken nose, heavy jaw, tired lower eyelids and short dark hair thinning at the crown. His default look measures whether somebody needs helping or hurting. A smile briefly makes him look younger, then he remembers where he is.

**Clothing and materials:** tobacco wool jacket with broad lapels, cream open collar and dark trousers. The jacket was expensive; elbows and pocket edges have worn smooth. Worn formal shoes stay conspicuously clean. Gold watch with a dead face, no oversized gangster jewelry. Wallet holds old staff identification; it is a close inspection/story prop, not a floating accessory.

**First person and motion:** cream cuff under a tobacco sleeve; readable broad hands and the gold watch. Weight shifts forward when threatened. He checks the person behind him before moving, squares his jacket after an indignity, and sometimes straightens furniture from habit. These gestures belong in safe windows; never lengthen a reload just to show personality. He helps an injured person efficiently and complains afterward.

**Personality:** loyalty is both his strength and the excuse he has used for violence. He remembers names and humiliations. His generosity can become control, especially with family. Good gift wrapping, bad monster films and unnecessary advice keep him human. He is not absolved because he is funny or paternal.

**Voice:** worn middle-low register, North Jersey English, clipped ends, audible breath when exhausted. Start threats conversationally. Anger compresses the sentence; extravagant outrage opens it up. Affection sounds like a practical complaint. The Keene confession drops the performance entirely. No stock mobster imitation, endless food references or phonetic accent spellings in subtitles.

**Audition contrast:** quiet “I left. That's the part I know.” against offended “You tore the lining. Do you know how hard that is to match?” The latter is a performance exercise, not a runtime damage claim without a matching visual event.

**Must survive simplification:** broad tobacco silhouette, broken-nose profile, cream collar, gold watch, protective stance. Fine wrinkles and individual hairs can wait.

## Evelyn “Eve” Vale — the woman who can command any room except herself

**Face and body:** 34, Black British from south London. Angular face, strong brow, short natural curls and an upright, deliberate carriage. Her eyes assess where attention is going. Long, precise gestures contrast with the way her hands suddenly become very still when Art is mentioned. Do not turn her composure into a permanently blank face.

**Clothing and materials:** asymmetrical ivory stage jacket, one black lapel and oxblood lining that flashes when she turns. High-waisted black trousers and low practical boots. Repaired cuffs are evidence of work, not decorative distress. Bare hands, a narrow dark wrist wrap, and a pencil or small practical tool placed where a performer could actually reach it. No compulsory top hat, cape, playing-card print or costume wand.

**First person and motion:** ivory cuffs and dark wrap. Idle hand movements suggest checking a seam or palming a small object; not continuous magic flourishes during combat. She chooses a sightline before entering a room. At a serious discovery the theatrical posture drops a little, making the unperformed person visible.

**Personality:** ambitious, exacting, funny at other people's expense, embarrassed by her need for approval. Her friendship with Art was damaged by her willingness to take the spotlight. Her growth is sharing credit and admitting what she cannot control. She can lose at dominoes to her mother without becoming less formidable.

**Voice:** rich, low smoky contralto; educated London delivery, warmer south London edges in private. Precise setups, brief strategic pauses and a hard final word. Big theatrical indignation should be occasional and earned. Real fear removes ornament: short requests, less air, no stage wink. Avoid copying a famous British performer or equating polish with emotional coldness.

**Audition contrast:** grand “For my next trick, a much smaller audience.” against nearly whispered “Play it again. And nobody speak.” Both should sound like the same woman protecting herself differently.

**Must survive simplification:** ivory/black asymmetric upper body, curls, strong brow, oxblood lining, bare hands and wrist wrap.

## Leon Mercer — the reliable man learning to leave something broken

**Face and body:** 39, Black American from southeast Texas. Lean build, long face, close-cropped hair and neat mustache. Work has left him with economical movements rather than exaggerated bulk. His resting expression is attentive, not confused. Let tiredness coexist with warmth.

**Clothing and materials:** faded teal coveralls open over a sand-colored shirt, sleeves rolled with practical consistency. Cloth is repaired at stress points; oil marks cluster where work would leave them. A repaired watch and two labeled keys, not an enormous belt of random tools. Boots look maintained, resoled and usable. His workwear should read differently from police or military gear.

**First person and motion:** rolled teal sleeves, sand shirt where appropriate, work-worn bare hands and repaired watch. He braces objects before applying force, listens to a malfunction and tests one thing at a time. Frustration starts in the jaw and finishes in an unusually exact sentence. Do not add slow tool pantomime to required interactions.

**Personality:** patient, proud of competence and increasingly aware that being indispensable is a way of avoiding home. He can lecture a machine as if it has disappointed him personally. His wife Della and daughter Ruthie are living people with lives beyond his guilt. Cheap detective stories, radio restoration and private singing matter as much as the old accident. He is neither gullible comic relief nor a secretly elite soldier.

**Voice:** warm middle register with grain and measured southeast Texas cadence. Natural contractions, clear consonants, comfortable pauses. He can build a sentence into a furious verdict without racing. In real danger he gives the useful instruction first; affection is unembarrassed. Do not generate a broad novelty drawl.

**Audition contrast:** pleased “There. Now it sounds like somebody meant it.” against tired “Home. Before I find another thing to fix.” Avoid putting the same gravel on him as Frankie.

**Must survive simplification:** lean teal silhouette, long face and mustache, rolled sleeves, two keys, patient working posture.

## Vivian Cross — the woman who can sell an escape she hasn't found

**Face and body:** 29, Mexican American from Los Angeles; born Vivian Cruz. Rounded face, strong nose, dark chin-length hair tucked behind one ear. Compact silhouette and very mobile expression. A smile can begin as a tactic and become real halfway through. Do not make her visual identity depend on sexualized proportions or impossible footwear.

**Clothing and materials:** narrow plum suit, pale patterned shirt, tan gloves, flat shoes and a convincing-looking but cheap gold bracelet. Tailoring is more important than the expense of the cloth. Seams and pockets support concealed papers. Gloves conceal trembling as well as keeping up appearances. The bracelet can show a little wear in close-up; its actual story value remains in writing, not a required collectible.

**First person and motion:** plum sleeve, tan glove, glimpse of bracelet. She checks exits casually enough to pretend she wasn't checking. She pats a pocket to confirm possession after a theft. When she returns to help somebody, let the quick movement read as a decision, not a flirtatious pose.

**Personality:** fast, shameless, observant and furious when someone sells her the same trick she uses. She enjoys generosity when she controls its meaning. Her brother Tomas's ruined livelihood cannot be fixed solely by sending money. She respects craft, tips too much, resents tiny fees and believes bad racing advice. Her humanity includes actual selfishness; every joke should not reveal a hidden saint.

**Voice:** bright middle register with a little husk; Los Angeles English. Conspiratorial warmth, quick pivots and musical salesmanship. A threat can keep the smile while losing the warmth. Sincerity is flatter and less polished. Do not infer a Spanish accent from heritage or add random Spanish as identity decoration. Her voice must remain distinct from Eve's careful stage cadence.

**Audition contrast:** delighted “Oh, sweetheart. You've just financed a terrible evening.” against angry-at-herself “I had a clean way out. You'd better be worth this.”

**Must survive simplification:** plum fitted silhouette, dark bob, strong nose, tan hands/gloves, quick exit-aware movement.

## Vincent Voss — hospitality as ownership

**Face and body:** apparent age around 58, American. Tall, upright, controlled stillness. Long face, heavy eyes, swept-back silvering hair and a broad practiced smile that can remain while the eyes become hostile. His threat comes from attention and certainty, not supernatural facial deformity. He should look like someone guests once felt honored to meet.

**Clothing and materials:** midnight-blue dinner jacket, cream shirt, restrained black bow tie and one signet ring. Wool and silk contrast subtly; avoid covering him in gold. Immaculate clothes imply other people absorb the consequences of his evening. He occupies space without fidgeting.

**Gesture and presence:** an open hand invites; the same hand staying extended too long becomes a demand. He remembers a preference, uses a name and holds the silence until somebody answers. Public anger starts as disappointed etiquette. Genuine loss of control is rare enough to matter. Most initial presence is voice over PA; a full animated body is not a prerequisite for the first story slice.

**Personality:** capable of genuine kindness and genuine attachment, but turns both into debts. He knowingly accepted collection to keep prosperity and control. His failed marriage to Helena exposes the limit of buying everything somebody likes. Cheap taffy and bad humming give private texture without turning him into a harmless eccentric. He can mourn somebody and still make the same ruthless choice again.

**Voice:** rich baritone, cultivated American with a light mid-Atlantic formality. Public host: rounded vowels, gracious pace, an audible smile. Private threat: quieter, more exact, fewer decorative words. Never constant trailer growl or shouting. The frightening moment is when courtesy becomes specific to a person's weakness.

**Audition contrast:** “My friends. An unpleasant interruption.” followed, in a separate private read, by “Read the small print, Vivian. I had you in mind.” Keep clean voice masters; PA treatment is a separate mix layer.

**Asset continuity:** the implemented cashier proprietor painting is **not confirmed to depict Voss**. Its older styling does not change the 1976 setting. If a future prop explicitly depicts him, match the Voss design; do not silently relabel the existing portrait. See [portrait provenance](../portrait-art/README.md).

**Must survive simplification:** tall still posture, midnight blue, silver hair, heavy eyes, welcoming hand and controlled smile.

## Edwin Marlowe — a witness who has made himself useful to the wrong people

**Face and body:** appears around 63, English, near Leeds. Narrow, slightly stooped frame, receding gray hair, prominent ears and an attentive face. His actual age and supernatural condition remain open. Do not introduce ghost transparency, ageless skin or an undead wound as an asset shortcut.

**Clothing and materials:** white shirt with rolled sleeves, burgundy waistcoat and old name badge. Working cloth has pressure wear, not fashionable distress. An ordinary bar towel gives his hands a task; it is not a magical artifact. His clothing should suggest maintenance and routine rather than theatrical servant livery.

**Gesture and presence:** works while listening, stops when something truly matters. A glass set down carefully can punctuate a confession. Avoid an endless wiping animation making him oblivious to nearby danger. His practical courtesy can feel absurd amid violence, but he recognizes the people in front of him.

**Personality:** remembers preferences and discrepancies, avoids decisions by remaining useful. Small acts of protection coexist with years of smoothing over suspicious departures. His sister Elsie's invitation home is ordinary and unresolved. Crosswords, biscuits and unashamed bad singing give him a life beyond hint delivery. He is not secretly a master assassin or all-knowing supernatural guide by default.

**Voice:** airy, dry middle-low register; light northern English / softened Yorkshire vowels. Plain phrasing, patient space, understatement without signaling the joke. Serious care sounds blunt, not mysteriously cryptic. His confession should contain shame and irritation with himself. Avoid making him whisper every line.

**Audition contrast:** “You've brought most of yourself back. That's a start.” against “Because I hoped you'd find a way out that didn't involve me.”

**Must survive simplification:** narrow burgundy silhouette, prominent ears, gray hair, white sleeves, hands that stop working when he chooses to speak honestly.

## Browser asset and voice production handoff

Start in Blender with neutral turnarounds and a lineup under the actual casino lighting. Build readable body and clothing masses before skin microdetail. Reuse a suitable rig where proportions allow; preserve silhouette differences rather than scaling one identical body. Match visible first-person sleeves, skin/gloves and accessories to third-person identity. Characters initially share gameplay capabilities.

Review a four-player combat scene before choosing final mesh, texture and animation budgets. Prioritize silhouette, movement, hands and readable face over cinematic lip sync. Hair can use economical solid forms or cards after profiling; no strand-hair requirement. Fine cloth simulation and full facial rigs are later polish. No numerical budgets are asserted as tested here.

For each character audition three identity candidates on the same five situations: quiet introduction, dry joke, urgent call, fear and confession. Include paired exchanges to catch voices that merge in a busy mix. Judge first without visuals. Accent, pitch, rhythm, emotional change and intelligibility all matter; pitch differences alone do not make a cast.

Final voice identities remain unselected. The one Voss/Callum pilot is an audition artifact, not canonical casting. Use [voice and dialogue](voice-and-dialogue.md) for generation/access status and manifests. This document authorizes design direction, not an automatic paid generation batch.
